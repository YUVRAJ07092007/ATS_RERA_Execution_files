## Subject

**Objection and gap analysis showing that the ATS/Hasta/Homekraft joint affidavit-cum-undertaking dated 08.06.2026 is only a generic regulatory undertaking before DTCP, and is neither compliance nor substantial compliance of the HARERA order dated 18.04.2024 in Complaint No. 4463 of 2021.**

---

## 1. Core objection in brief

1.1. The impugned affidavit is projected as if it protects the rights of the decree-holder/allottee. In substance, however, it merely contains broad, future-oriented, non-specific assurances before DTCP. It does not contain any unit-specific compliance, payment proof, possession timeline, construction-status disclosure, escrow disclosure, or binding execution undertaking in favour of Unit No. **3051**, Tower No. **03**.

1.2. The affidavit is not filed as a compliance affidavit in the pending HARERA execution. It is filed before DTCP for obtaining or supporting regulatory benefit relating to revised plans / change of developer. Therefore, it cannot be treated as compliance with the binding HARERA order dated **18.04.2024**.

1.3. The affidavit is materially deficient because it omits key facts already recorded in **sections 3 and 4** below, including the expired compliance periods, pending execution, payment and disclosure defaults, and the absence of a binding possession plan.

1.4. The affidavit further contains a reservation that compliance is subject to **“attainment of finality in pending appeal, if any”** — see **section 4.1** below.

1.5. The affidavit, therefore, should be rejected as compliance. At best, it may be treated as an admission that ATS Realworth Pvt. Ltd., Hasta Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd., and Homekraft Infra Pvt. Ltd. are aware of the decree-holder’s rights but are seeking regulatory advantage without first executing the HARERA order in full.

## 4. Specific deficiencies in the impugned affidavit

### 4.1. Harmful reservation: “subject to finality in pending appeal, if any”

The affidavit’s reference to compliance being subject to **“attainment of finality in pending appeal, if any”** is the most serious weakness.

This clause is objectionable because:

- it attempts to make a binding and executable order conditional;
- it may be used by ATS/Hasta/Homekraft to postpone payment until appeal proceedings are concluded;
- it contradicts the pendency of execution proceedings;
- it is inconsistent with the decree-holder’s crystallised rights;
- it creates ambiguity where the affidavit should have given unconditional assurance.

**Necessary correction:** Any affidavit relied upon before DTCP/HARERA must state that the HARERA order is **presently executable** unless specifically stayed by a competent forum, and that no appeal, if any, shall operate as a stay unless a specific stay order is produced.

---

### 4.2. No identification of the decree-holder, unit, complaint, or order

The affidavit uses generic terminology such as **“Decree Holder(s)”** but does not specifically identify:

- Mr. **Vijyant Agarwal**;
- Mrs. **Vineeta Agarwal**;
- **Unit No. 3051**, Tower No. **03**, Type-D;
- ATS Grandstand Phase-I, Sector 99A, Gurugram;
- Complaint No. **4463 of 2021**;
- HARERA order dated **18.04.2024**;
- Section 39 rectification order dated **08.08.2024**;
- the pending execution proceedings.

**Consequence:** A generic affidavit cannot be used to defeat, dilute, postpone, or satisfy a unit-specific executable order.

---

### 4.3. No undertaking of direct pre-EMI payment to the financial institution

The HARERA order dated **18.04.2024** directs that pre-EMI/EMI shall be paid **directly to the financial institution** till offer of possession. The impugned affidavit merely states that pre-EMI shall be paid in a “time-bound manner” but does not undertake or confirm such **direct payment to the financial institution** as required by the RERA order.

**Consequence:** A vague promise of “time-bound” pre-EMI payment does not satisfy the binding direction of **direct payment to the financial institution** under the HARERA order.

---

### 4.4. No definite possession date; no rejection of the 2031 extension

The allottee’s case is that the adjudicated / contractual possession timeline has already expired, and the due date is **30.06.2023**. ATS’s alleged revised projection of **30.06.2031** amounts to an unilateral eight-year extension.

The affidavit does not:

- admit the original/adjudicated due date of **30.06.2023**;
- explain the delay from 2023 to 2026;
- reject the unilateral extension to **30.06.2031**;
- give a legally binding possession date for Unit No. 3051;
- give a tower-wise completion schedule for Tower No. 03;
- undertake that paper possession shall not stop pre-EMI/delay liability;
- undertake that possession shall mean lawful physical possession after OC/CC, essential services, fire safety, lift, water, electricity, road access, parking, and common-area readiness.

**Consequence:** The affidavit gives no meaningful protection against further indefinite delay.

## 5. Why this affidavit cannot be treated as compliance

5.1. **Compliance requires performance, not promise.**  
A party cannot satisfy a binding order merely by stating that it will protect rights in future. Compliance must be shown through proof of payment, proof of disclosure, proof of construction status, proof of escrow records, and a definite possession plan.

5.2. **A regulatory affidavit cannot substitute execution.**  
DTCP proceedings relating to revised building plan / change of developer do not replace HARERA execution proceedings. The decree-holder’s executable rights cannot be displaced by a document filed before another authority for a different purpose.
